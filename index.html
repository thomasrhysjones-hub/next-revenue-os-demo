          Pass / fail criteria

          Revenue results, not AI activity

          Commercial success: does additional gross profit from existing accounts outweigh estimated foregone new-business profit and the cost of the pilot?

          Measures: signed gross profit, margins, buyer-confirmed cross-sell and upsell, renewals when observable, and conversion rates.

          Guardrails: no unapproved pricing, rights or promises; protect privacy and editorial independence; monitor buyer experience.

          Comparison: check pilot results against past performance and comparable non-pilot accounts. Ninety days provides directional evidence, not proof of long-term renewal uplift.
          Transparency by design

          What is working, what is simulated, and where AI can fail

          Working offline

          Editable fictional enquiries, repeatable deterministic routing, structured gaps, package hypotheses, red-team flags, draft replies, mandatory seller gates, radar examples and pilot calculator.

          Actual AI hand-off

          A user can copy a structured prompt into ChatGPT, import model JSON, validate it and apply a reviewed result. Three recorded ChatGPT responses are bundled as reproducible examples, not live API calls. It is not an integrated API or an autonomous agent.

          Not claimed

          No live NEXT inventory, CRM, contact identity checks, domain authentication, LinkedIn/company/news retrieval, sponsor lists, prices, attendance data, audience guarantees, model quality benchmarks, forecast uplift, production security or verified partner suitability.

          Known failure modes

          • Naive geo matching can read “send us packages” as the US. This implementation only recognises explicit US geography.
          • A simple scanner can flag “cannot guarantee” as a promise. Guardrails try to recognise negation, but context remains a weakness.
          • Structured JSON validation cannot prove that statements are true. A model can still invent rates, availability, fit, or rights.
          • Pricing examples are publicly listed, but not necessarily applicable to the chosen event or date.
          • Success benchmarks and test outcomes cannot be estimated credibly without NEXT baseline data.

          Who is accountable? Commercial Operations owns source-of-truth content, policy, audit and integrations. The seller owns the buyer, final message and negotiation. Sales Director owns adoption, coaching, selective strategic deals, escalations and commercial results.
